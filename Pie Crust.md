## Notes

- **Make ahead and freezing:** prepare the dough through step 5 and freeze the discs for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before using.
- **Salt:** use regular table salt. If using kosher salt, use 1 and 1/4 teaspoons.
- **Shortening:** the butter gives flakiness and flavor, and the shortening's high melting point helps the crust hold its shape. Butter-flavor shortening is fine. For no shortening, the author points to her all-butter pie crust. Some readers have used lard instead of shortening with success.
- **Food processor:** it can bring the dough together in step 1, but the author finds it quickly overworks the dough and recommends a pastry cutter.
- **Dry dough cracking at the edges when rolling:** use enough ice water. Working the fats in too much makes the dough feel too wet before enough water goes in, leaving it dry and thirsty. Keep the crumbly texture of the fats. If the edges already crack, dip your fingers in ice-cold water, meld the edges back together, wait a minute, and roll again.
- **Dough crumbling when rolling:** usually too much fat and not enough flour and water, often from overworking the fat or ingredients that weren't cold enough; refrigerate the dry ingredients first. If it's too late, sprinkle a little ice water and flour onto the cracks, work it in gently, wait a minute, and roll again.
- **More crusts:** make another separate batch. Doubling or tripling the recipe leads to over- or under-working the dough.
