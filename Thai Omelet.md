Eggs beaten with fish sauce, lime juice and a little rice flour, then poured from a height into hot oil so they puff up with ragged, crisp edges.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient                      | Half batch | As written (1 serving) | Double batch |
| ------------------------------- | ---------- | ---------------------- | ------------ |
| Large eggs                      | 1          | 2                      | 4            |
| Lime juice or plain vinegar     | ¼ teaspoon | ½ teaspoon             | 1 teaspoon   |
| Thai fish sauce                 | ½ teaspoon | 1 teaspoon             | 2 teaspoons  |
| Water                           | ½ tablespoon | 1 tablespoon         | 2 tablespoons |
| Rice flour or cornstarch        | ½ tablespoon | 1 tablespoon         | 2 tablespoons |
| Plain vegetable oil             | ⅜–½ cup    | ¾–1 cup                | 1½–2 cups    |

## Instructions

1. Combine the eggs, lime juice or vinegar, fish sauce, water, and rice flour or cornstarch in a medium bowl. Beat with a fork until frothy. If the flour forms a few lumps, break up as many as you can with your fingers.
2. Heat the vegetable oil in a small pot or a round-bottom wok over medium-high heat until lightly smoking. Hold the bowl about a foot above the pan and pour the egg mixture into the oil in one go.
3. The egg mixture will puff up immediately. Don't disturb it. After 20 seconds, flip the omelet — there's no need to keep it neatly round, asymmetrical edges are what you want. Cook the other side for another 20 seconds, then remove from the pan and serve immediately.

## Notes

- Special equipment: a wok or saucier.
